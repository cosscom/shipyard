**Help → Keyboard shortcuts** (⌘/) lists every shortcut, one line each, and
**Settings → Shortcuts** searches them by name or by keys. In the Mac app
most are in the menu bar too, under File, View, Go and Help. They work
everywhere in the window, terminals included: the app sees them first.

In the Linux app Ctrl stands in for ⌘. In a terminal there, Ctrl and a
letter go to the shell (Ctrl+W, Ctrl+D, Ctrl+K…); add Shift for the app's
(Ctrl+Shift+T, Ctrl+Shift+K). Ctrl+Shift+C and Ctrl+Shift+V copy and paste
there.

## Your keyboard's layout

Back and Forward are ⌘ and the two keys right of P, wherever those are on
your keyboard. On a US layout they read ⌘[ and ⌘]. On a layout where [ and ]
need ⌥, the app shows the keys in that place instead, on the arrows'
tooltips, in the Keyboard shortcuts sheet, in Settings and in ⌘K: ⌘Å and ⌘¨
on a Norwegian keyboard, ⌘Ü and ⌘+ on a German one. The Mac app asks macOS
which input source is in use, and asks again when its window comes to the
front, so switching the input source shows there too. Until the app knows
your layout, it shows a US keyboard's keys.

The ⌘⌥ shortcuts with a letter (⌘⌥D, ⌘⌥C, ⌘⌥S, ⌘⌥I) go by the key's place
too, because ⌥ changes what a letter types. The others go by the character
you type, so they read the same on any layout.

## What they do where

- **Back** (⌘[) and **Forward** (⌘]) take you through the places you've
  been, as in a browser: a page such as the Agent Dashboard, or a worktree
  and the tab you had open in it. A mouse's back and forward buttons do the
  same. In a File tab's editor, ⌘[ and ⌘] stay its outdent and indent. In
  the Linux app's terminals they're Ctrl+Shift+[ and Ctrl+Shift+], because
  Ctrl+[ is esc to a shell.
- **Go to file** (⌘P) finds a file in the worktree in front, with what the
  agent changed this turn first. ↵ opens it in a File tab, ⌥↵ beside the
  pane, and ⌘↵ in your editor.
- **Save file** (⌘S) saves the File tab you're in. If the agent wrote the
  file since you opened it, you choose what to keep instead.
- **Files panel** (⌘⇧E) shows or hides the worktree's files beside its
  tabs: all of them, or what the agent changed this turn.
- **Close group** (⌘⇧W) closes the tab group in front; its worktree and
  agents carry on. With one group, it closes the window.
- **Rename worktree** (F2), on a worktree in the sidebar or the rail, gives
  it a display name. ↵ keeps it and esc leaves it as it was.
- **Move between panes** (⌘⌥ and an arrow) works from a terminal too. A
  terminal keeps ⇥ for itself.
- **Find in the chat** (⌘F) finds words in the chat you're in. ↵ or ⌘G goes
  to the next one, and esc closes it.
- **Send the reply** (↵) sends a reply box; ⇧↵ is a new line. **Stop the
  agent** (esc) in a chat's reply box, while its agent works, stops it as
  esc does at its terminal.
- **Fold a project** (← and →) on a project in the sidebar hides or shows
  its worktrees. **Open a row's menu** (⇧F10) on a sidebar row or a tab
  opens the menu a right-click does.
- **Console and network** (⌘⌥I) in a Browser tab shows or hides the page's
  Console and Network drawer.
- With [Labs](/guides/labs) on, **Compare lanes** (⌥1–5) in a Compare tab
  pick its Chat, Diff, Preview, Terminal or Artifacts lane, and **Swap
  compared sides** (⌘⌥S) swaps its two sides.
